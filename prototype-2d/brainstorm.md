# Brainstorm: Echoes of Keru

## The core feeling

Cold stone, warm light, and the surprise that a ruin can feel alive. The penguin should move with a little more grace than expected: waddle on the ground, launch into a clean jump, then turn the air dash into a last-second save.

## Mechanics to explore

- **Lantern shards**: collectibles are pieces of a broken navigation lantern. Bringing them together changes the level lighting and reveals hidden platforms.
- **Snow-slide**: hold down on a slope to tuck into a fast slide. Low tunnels become shortcuts, not just obstacles.
- **Echo jump**: touching certain glyphs leaves a temporary “ghost” jump that can be triggered once later in the room.
- **Tide clock**: the ruins slowly flood from left to right. It is a gentle pressure, not an instant-death timer.
- **Friendly hazards**: sleepy stone guardians, bell crabs, and gusts from underground vents make movement interesting without turning the game into combat.
- **Postcard camera**: optional snapshots at high overlooks can pin discoveries to a field journal.

## Level sketch

1. **The Drowned Steps** — learn movement around broken stairs and shallow tide pools.
2. **The Bell Gallery** — vertical platforming; bells ring when the penguin lands near them and reveal the next route.
3. **The Blue Archive** — a library of ice tablets where the room rotates around the player.
4. **The Observatory** — a long final climb with constellations that only appear when all lantern shards are reunited.

## Questions worth testing next

- Is the double-jump more fun as a reliable second hop or as a resource that recharges at glyphs?
- Does the penguin need a wall-slide / wall-jump to make the vertical rooms feel playful?
- Should the lantern be a permanent light source, or should carrying it force a tradeoff with movement speed?
- What is the smallest number of verbs that lets a player feel clever in a room?

## Visual direction

Keep the palette mostly deep ocean teal, lichen green, and old parchment gold. The penguin sprite is the sharpest, most readable point in each scene; ruins should be broad silhouettes with a few precise glyphs rather than noisy tiles.
