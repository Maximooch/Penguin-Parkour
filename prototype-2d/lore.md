# Lore: The Keru Below

Long before the ice reached the southern coast, the people of Keru built their city beneath the glacier line. They were not conquerors. They were listeners. Their towers were tuned to the movements of the sea, and their observatories recorded the lights that moved under the water at night.

The city was abandoned in one evening.

No battle marks the stone. No graves have been found. Every door was simply left open, every lantern extinguished at once. The only surviving record is a phrase repeated across the city in a script that resembles a map:

> When the moon sinks, return the light to the listening dark.

## The expedition

Our penguin is a courier from the rookery at the edge of the thaw. A storm exposes the top of an old Keru stairwell, and a small, stubborn glow drifts up from below. The rookery’s elders call it a bad omen. The penguin calls it a map.

The first lantern shard is found in the drowned steps. The second is held in a sunken archive. The third is carried by the ruins’ own memory: a moving constellation that appears only when someone crosses the old paths with enough courage.

## The old light

The lantern is not a tool made by the Keru. It is a promise they made with the dark beneath the city. When the three shards are reunited, the lantern does not illuminate the ruins. It illuminates the spaces between them — the routes, stories, and small lives that were always there.

## Creatures and people

- **The rookery** — a practical, close-knit colony that trades in fish, buttons, and excellent weather gossip.
- **The bell crabs** — tiny crabs that live in the old signaling channels. They are attracted to movement and dislike being thanked.
- **The stone sleepers** — guardian statues whose job was to remember every visitor. They wake slowly and have never agreed on what counts as trespassing.
- **The tide beneath** — not water exactly, but a patient underground current that carries sound, light, and sometimes lost objects back to where they belong.

## Tone

Keru should feel mysterious without being grim. The ancient world is strange, but it is not cruel. A broken mechanism might still be trying to help. A guardian might be lonely. The penguin’s curiosity is the key that makes the place kind again.
